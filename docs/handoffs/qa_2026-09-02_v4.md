## Summary

Ran the full gesture matrix Kazim asked for against Natalia's rebuilt plate wheel
(`engineering_2026-09-02_v7.md`), on a production build at :9157, at 1280x900 and 1440x900 for the
wheel and 1024x768 and 375x812 with real touch. **All eleven rows pass.** Three defects were found
and fixed at the source during the gate, and every affected row was re-run afterwards.

**Real input, not synthetic.** Natalia flagged that `browse` has no `Input.dispatchMouseEvent` in
its CDP allowlist, so her drag proof was synthetic PointerEvents. It has no `Emulation.*` either.
This pass drove the Playwright Chromium bundled with gstack directly from `bun`, which gives real
`Input.dispatchMouseEvent`, real `Input.dispatchTouchEvent`, real
`Emulation.setEmulatedMedia` for reduced motion, and real `Performance.getMetrics`. Playwright's
actionability check is what cannot hover a moving element, not its input layer, so every hover and
click here is a raw-coordinate `mouse.move` with the wheel left turning rather than frozen first.

**Kazim's two original bugs are genuinely gone.** Listening for `dragstart`, `drag`, `dragend`,
`drop` and `selectstart` in the capture phase across a real 300px mouse drag with 60 moves returned
`[]`: nothing fired at all. No download in any test. And the wheel follows the hand on the angle to
within **0.01 degrees** (22.74 measured against a 22.75 pointer sweep), with the grabbed plate
staying on the pointer's own ray from the hub (-134.64 against -134.65).

**What I fixed:**

1. **The wheel stopped dead mid-throw and stayed stopped (high).** Throw it and let go without
   moving your hand: the plates slide out from under the motionless pointer, which fires
   `pointerleave` on the hub even though the hand never moved. That lifted the hover suspension, the
   next plate arrived under the same still pointer, and the hover pause swallowed the throw.
   Reproduced deterministically at 1440: **2657 ms of dead standstill, final speed 0.00 deg/s**, in
   2 of 4 runs. Never at 1280, which is why it survived the last pass: it needs a plate to leave the
   pointer before the next one arrives, and that is phase and geometry. Now only a real
   `pointermove` lifts the suspension. After: 8 runs of 8 across both widths, **0 flat frames**,
   back at ambient.

2. **A slow drag ended in a dead beat (medium).** Releasing with the hand already at rest set the
   wheel's speed to exactly nothing and crept it back over about a second and a half:
   **5 frames, 42.5 ms of standstill, 0.00 deg/s at +50 ms**. A throwless release now hands the wheel
   straight back to the ambient turn. After: **0 flat frames, 2.20 deg/s averaged over the first
   0.3 s** (was 0.57).

3. **The keyboard focus ring landed below the fold (high, accessibility).** A visitor already
   looking at the About band who tabs forward into the wheel landed on the first plate with its ring
   at **top 916 in a 900px viewport, 3 runs of 3** at human tab speed, and the page never recovered.
   Two causes: Chrome scrolls a newly focused element into view by the smallest amount that reveals
   it *where it is*, and does so *after* `focusin`, while the wheel is about to move that plate up to
   a full diameter away (hub at 403 when `focusin` fires, at 967 half a second later); and Lenis owns
   this page's scroll, so a native `scrollIntoView` issued while Lenis has an animation in flight
   settles between the two (landed at scrollY 268 instead of 574, 3 of 3). Fixed with a small
   `lib/scroller.ts` that publishes the Lenis instance, so anything moving the page asks the scroll
   owner instead of racing it, plus a correction armed on keyboard focus and run once the browser's
   own scroll has landed. After: **all six plates fully on screen at both widths**, 9 entry paths of
   9. A mouse click and a mouse drag still move the page by **0 px**.

Headline numbers: ambient 2.14 to 2.17 deg/s everywhere against a 2.143 target; 0.00 degrees of
drift while hovered or focused; resume within two frames; zero horizontal leak at 375/768/1280/1920;
zero console errors; zero plate collisions with the About copy or the "Fresh always" column across a
**full revolution driven by real drags**, 72 of 72 five-degree buckets covered, closest approach 58
px at 1280 and 80 px at 1440; **zero layouts and zero frames over 16.7 ms** during a 2 s drag and a
2 s throw, avg 8.33 ms, zero long tasks.

`tsc --noEmit` clean, `next build` clean. Production server rebuilt and **left running on :9157**.

## Risks

1. **A reverse throw passes through zero for 40 to 110 ms.** Throwing the wheel against its ambient
   direction means it has to decelerate through a standstill before resuming forwards. Measured 41
   to 109 ms under 0.25 deg/s at the crossing over six runs. This is a direction change, not a stall,
   and the dwell is set by `AMBIENT / BOOST_DECAY_S` = 2.143 / 0.45 = 4.76 deg/s squared. Shortening
   it means shortening `BOOST_DECAY_S`, which would cut every forward throw short too. Forward
   throws and parked releases both show zero flat frames, so the wheel is never left stopped.
   Recorded rather than changed. Kazim's call, one constant.

2. **D1 was width-dependent and only showed at 1440.** The same class of bug can hide at any
   untested width. The mechanism is now removed rather than tuned, so I do not expect a second
   instance, but anything future that clears a suspension from a *content-driven* event rather than
   a hand-driven one will reintroduce it.

3. **`lib/scroller.ts` is a module singleton.** It holds one Lenis instance for the whole app, which
   is correct for a single-page site with one `SmoothScroll` mounted, and would need revisiting if
   there were ever two.

4. **The 500 ms focus-scroll settle is a timer, not an event.** There is no reliable cross-browser
   signal for "the browser has finished its own scroll-into-view and Lenis has eased it". 500 ms
   held in 9 runs of 9 across three tab speeds and both entry paths, but it is a constant tuned
   against Chrome and this page's Lenis duration of 1.05 s.

5. **Not tested, honestly:** a real browser tab switch (Playwright pins pages visible; four ways of
   forcing it were tried, so the handler and its `document.hidden` read were exercised via an
   overridden getter plus a real `visibilitychange`, but the browser's own switch was not); a real
   `pointercancel` (dispatched synthetically, since no mouse gesture makes one and touch is excluded
   from the drag by design); real hardware, a physical mouse, trackpad or touchscreen; the OS-level
   reduced-motion setting rather than the emulated media query; screen readers; Safari and Firefox.

6. **Out of matrix, seen in an artifact:** at 375, mid-scroll, the fixed Cosmos wordmark sits over
   the About paragraph text (`mobile-row-375.png`). Lesson 17's drop shadow is doing some work but
   the overlap reads as legible-ish rather than clean. Not touched. Worth a look on the next design
   pass.
