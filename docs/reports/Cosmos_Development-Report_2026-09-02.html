Today

What we delivered

In one build day the Cosmos Burger website went from Lorena's Canva blueprint to a finished page you can click through. Every band she drew is now real: the hero with the animated Cosmos logo, the Unleash the Flavor story with the burger plates cascading down the side, the three values, the best sellers carousel, the menu and order pop-ups, catering, the four food halls, the reviews and the footer. It is built in Cosmos Burger's own colours, magenta, yellow, purple and cream, not in a template, and the copy is Lorena's word for word. It has been tested at nine screen sizes, checked for accessibility, and put through a security review. Where content has not reached us yet, the site tells the truth rather than guessing: no invented prices, and no button that leads nowhere.

The two pop-ups

CHECK OUT OUR MENU opens a menu pop-up with the items grouped by category, burgers, chicken sandwiches, hot dog, tenders and sides. It carries no prices, because no menu has been supplied yet, so it is a browsing aid rather than a menu today. ORDER ONLINE opens the ordering pop-up listing all five locations with their Toast and DoorDash options. The two links that are public today, DoorDash for Carlsbad and Oceanside, work. The rest read Online ordering coming soon rather than opening an empty page, and the moment you send the links they become live buttons.

One thing worth saying plainly. Nothing on this site is invented. Prices, ordering links and contact details that have not reached us are shown as coming soon, so a guest is never sent to a dead page and never told a price you did not set.
Quality

How we checked it

The site was tested in a real browser, not assumed to work. Every page was walked at nine screen sizes, from the smallest phone still in use to a wide desktop, including phones held sideways.

Nothing slid off the side of the screen at any size, on any page, and no button was too small to tap. Five issues were found during the check and all five were fixed the same day, the most visible being the logo sitting on top of a yellow headline once the page had been scrolled, and location cards that were invisible but could still be reached by keyboard.

Readable for everyone

Text and background colours were measured against the international accessibility standard and every combination passes. The whole site can be used with a keyboard alone, with a visible outline showing where you are, and the pop-ups behave properly for screen reader users. Every photo carries a description.

Respects motion settings

Guests who have asked their phone or computer to reduce motion get the site completely still, with nothing animating and nothing hidden. We tested that with the setting genuinely switched on.

Works without extras

With scripts switched off the page still shows all of its content, so search engines and older devices see the whole story. No errors were reported by the browser on any page.

Security

The site collects nothing, has no accounts, no forms and no tracking cookies, so there is nothing on it for anyone to steal, and we still ran a full security review and set every protective setting a modern browser looks for, including the one gap the review found.

Over to you

What we need from you

Five things, batched so you can answer them in one go. None of them blocks a preview, and every one of them turns a coming soon into a finished piece of the site.

  1. Oceanside, in or outOceanside appears in the ordering pop-up but not in the Locations section of the blueprint. We followed the blueprint exactly. Should Oceanside also get its own location card?
  2. The menu, with pricesA PDF or a list with descriptions and prices. The menu pop-up is built and waiting for real content.
  3. Toast and DoorDash links, per hallWe have public DoorDash links for Carlsbad and Oceanside. We need Toast and DoorDash for San Clemente at Miramar, Little Italy at Global Fork, and UCSD Campus at Station 8.
  4. Contact detailsWhat the footer should show: a phone number, an email, and an address if you want one. It was left blank in the brief, so the footer currently shows the social links only.
  5. The Horizon fontHorizon is a paid Adobe font. Does Tiger Hospitality Group have a licence for it? The site currently uses a very close free stand in. If you have the licence we swap it in one step, and if not the stand in stays and the headlines read a touch narrower than the Canva file.
